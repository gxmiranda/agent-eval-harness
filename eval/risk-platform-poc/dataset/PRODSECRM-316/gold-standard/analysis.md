## Executive Summary [AI-GENERATED]

Red Hat has granted a time-boxed exception letting OpenShift Container Platform (OCP) z-stream releases skip a release-pipeline check that verifies each container image's product identifier (CPE) matches its security advisory's CPE, because OCP still ships mixed RHEL 8/RHEL 9 images under one advisory. This is not hypothetical: live verification of Red Hat's own published vulnerability data (VEX) for CVE-2025-65637 confirms RHEL 8 images are already misattributed to an RHEL 9 product entry — a live-fetch-confirmed false-positive risk to CPE-first vulnerability scanners today, with a real risk of customer-trust exposure if a future misattribution goes the more severe, unquantified direction and could result in a customer delaying remediation of a genuinely vulnerable image reported as already fixed. Severity is rated High primarily on independently-verified governance grounds, not on the one confirmed technical instance: both permanent fixes (ART-14748, KONFLUX-6854) remain undated and unfunded, and the parent engineering program has slipped its delivery date three times and was recently deprioritized — raising real doubt the exception will be resolved, rather than renewed, by its 2026-10-03 expiration. The single most important action is to require a committed remediation date on ART-14748 or KONFLUX-6854 as a hard condition of any renewal, and to correctly label this as a Conforma exception so it enters standard governance review. PSRR escalation is not currently recommended, but should be revisited as soon as a renewal decision is under consideration — not only after a renewal is granted — if no committed remediation date is attached by then.

### Rating Rationale

**Severity justification:**
High severity is warranted by three compounding factors, not any single one: (1) the defect is not theoretical — it is documented as already present in a published, customer-facing security artifact (the CVE-2025-65637 VEX document); (2) the org has a track record, evidenced in KONFLUX-11654, of OCP image label defects shipping to production without the release being halted, which undercuts confidence that the "name and timestamp checks remain enforced" compensating control in this exception will reliably catch adjacent problems; and (3) the two permanent remediation paths are both stalled (see Likelihood/Section 4), meaning this is not a short, contained gap. It is not Critical because the exposure is explicitly bounded to advisory/CSAF metadata — the actual container images ship with correct content and correct per-image CPE labels. **[Reconciled on adversarial review round 6]:** this structural argument (metadata-only, correct digests) is necessary but not sufficient on its own -- it establishes the *category* of harm is bounded (not a wrong-bits-shipped defect), but does not by itself rule out Critical *within* that category. The full "not Critical today" determination also depends on the population-sweep caveat in the ratings table above (the sweep performed is incomplete in exactly the way that matters, so this is a "clean so far, not a confident ceiling" conclusion, not a purely structural one). Both statements are true and non-contradictory: the metadata/digest-integrity argument bounds the *type* of harm, while the incomplete-sweep caveat bounds the *confidence* in "not Critical" within that type.

**Likelihood justification:**
The event that defines this risk (a mismatched CPE appearing in a published advisory) is already recurring, not speculative — OCP has multiple z-streams in flight during the exception window (4.12-4.18 cited) and will continue triggering this condition on every mixed-stream release until either the exception expires or a permanent fix lands. Controls partially reducing likelihood of *harm* (as opposed to the metadata error itself): the scoped, OCP-only CI constraint (not a global override) and continued enforcement of name/timestamp checks. Controls that do not appear robust: reliance on downstream scanners to correctly handle a known-mismatched CPE, given ROX-27482's evidence that Konflux-image scanner accuracy already has open gaps.

**Impact justification [rating changed from Major to Moderate on adversarial review round 4]:**
Quantification is limited by available evidence — the ticket documents one concrete instance (CVE-2025-65637/RHSA-2026:6493), now independently verified against the live VEX document during adversarial review (confirmed direction: false-positive risk, not false-negative; see Severity Rating table above), but the analysis does not enumerate how many *other* CVEs or z-streams have been affected, how many customers consume the affected CSAF/VEX documents, or whether any customer has yet acted on a mismatched entry.
